### 🔴 Blocker

**1. Commit-to-publish window leaves a committed rule looking like a pending claim (C1 / K1', found by both).** `agents/core/src/guardian_spark_runtime.cpp:468-552` writes `keys_`/`rules_` and stages the "armed" audit under `registry_mu_` in step (1), releases the lock, and only pops the finished claims and publishes outcomes in step (3) under a fresh acquisition (`:588-646`). In that window the head claim is still the key's marker with no outcome. Two consequences, both verified: (a) `attach_rule`'s `key_claimed` (`:835-840`) sees the entry and QUEUES a new claim instead of joining the committed `keys_[K]`; the step-(3) refill dispatches it, `backend_->arm()` mints a second subscription for the deduplicated watcher (`spark_engine.cpp:774/:850`), `keys_.emplace(key, fresh)` at `:488` silently no-ops on the existing key, the rule commits against an untracked `PerKey`, and that subscription can never be disarmed (a permanent leak + a dead rule). (b) `detach_rule_locked`'s Case-0 (`:1035-1059`) matches the committed claim (kind, rule_id, `!withdrawn`, `!waiter_abandoned`, `!outcome` all still true; `index_held == false` is not consulted), marks a LIVE rule "withdrawn", returns without queuing a disarm, and leaves `keys_`/`rules_` intact. Anchor: design §R5.2 (one current claim per key; same-key siblings share one subscription) and #3816 (no leaked subscription). Unreachable today only because PR-1's waiter plus engine `mtx_` serialize callers; PR-2 removes exactly that wait, so this is a defect in the machinery PR-1 exists to build. Fix: on the adopted path, publish the success verdicts and pop the finished claims INSIDE step (1) under the same lock (the deferred publication is only needed while a compensating disarm is pending), treat `keys_.emplace(...).second == false` as a hard error, and make Case-0 skip a claim whose commit ran (`end == Committed` / `!index_held`). Falsifier: a test that parks the drain between step (1) and (3) (seam), issues a same-key attach and a same-rule detach in the window, and asserts one backend arm, one subscription, and a real disarm on detach.

**2. The subscription ownership guard is installed after fallible allocations (C2 / K5, found by both).** In `on_arm_complete`, `finished.push_back` (`:417-418`) and `live.push_back` (`:420-422`) run BEFORE `compensating = sub` (`:476`); a `bad_alloc` there reaches the outer catch (`:584`), step (3) finds `compensating` empty, and the live subscription `S` is never disarmed. The comment at `:469-475` claims the guard "exists BEFORE the fallible work"; it does not. Second window: `compensating.reset()` at `:518` precedes `stage()`'s `verdicts.emplace_back` (`:392/:520`); a throw there publishes "arm drain failed" for a rule that is committed and armed (a spurious failure the engine's defensive `detach_rule` then cleans up, so no leak, but a false verdict). Anchor: `docs/cpp-conventions.md` ownership/RAII; #3831's rollback-before-protected-mutation contract; policy floor (ownership/lifetime defect of the kind `cpp-safety` blocks). The existing fault test (`test_guardian_spark_runtime.cpp:4673-4727`) injects a throwing waker copy after the guard is set and covers neither window. Fix: assign `compensating` from `**r` as the first statement on entry (nothrow `optional<uint64_t>`), and `reserve()` `finished`/`live`/`verdicts` before any registry mutation so staging after adoption cannot throw. Falsifier: an allocation-fault seam before the first `push_back` with a successful arm; assert `disarms == 1` and no live `keys_` entry.
